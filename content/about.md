Hello!

You’re here, and you’re queer!

We established this resource space largely because of a central need that many of us will experience - navigating gender-affirming care in this city-state.

The pathways to care, and access to it, has been largely convoluted for many; for minors, the biggest uphill task has largely been navigating parents who are resistant - or worse, parents who have been sucked into the anti-trans rhetoric the world consistently pushes out.

The rest of us get Confused Doctor Syndrome - doctors clueless about where to send you. The healthcare system has the unusual potential to be pulled out from under us if one or two doctors decide to leave for private practice (which had been the trigger for the first guide, the guide that predated the Library).

We don’t know how healthcare will move. It is very hard for us to determine this - sometimes, it seems, there are glimmers of hope, only for bureaucratic process to stall, and despite us existing before this country even became independent, the processes that would accommodate our existence continue to be shrouded in bureaucratic fog, whispered from ear to ear, for fear of us making our existence too prominent and for society to resist. The hope is that, as the Transcend Library persists, and as we continue to be here, that things get easier; we will continually update these guides, and maintain them, so long as we have a team of people who can help write and compile what’s happening.

The nature of this resource space means that a lot of the discussions we have will revolve around the logistics of getting medical care, the various ways we can change endocrinological processes in our bodies, the literal reshaping of the body through surgical intervention, and the social and legal aspects of transitioning.

But the inherent nature of our identities, the metaphysical desire to reshape and change what was consigned to us at birth, means that having a doctor determine who you are and tell you who you should be is incredibly problematic.

That’s the terrifying part about transness - it is something you have to discover and decide on your own. The good news is that we enjoy way more autonomy than we’ve had before and that gender-affirming care is still way more accessible here than it would be in other areas; the bad news is that having this autonomy means you have to decide for yourself who you would want to be, because your identity is shaped, ultimately, by you.

The doctors don’t determine if you’re trans; the psychologists and counsellors don’t determine if you’re trans - *you do*. They can *help*, but decisions about transition, about your gender identity, is ultimately something you have to make yourself.

You might still be questioning, at a point where you’re thinking about being trans; or you might have had understood who you were for many, many years. Our journeys are vastly different and personal, and *no one can tell you any different.* So what if it’s just a feeling?

You’re you, and that’s terrifying, isn’t it?

Take this as a manifesto of sorts - our desire as librarians here has been and will always be to empower server members and the community to make decisions on their own, and to that end we will continue to work with the other trans community organisations in Singapore to create resource channels and document the tools that are available to you, so that it is no longer simply just whispered from ear to ear, from generation to generation, but easily accessible for anyone who wishes to embark on their journey of self-discovery, of gender affirmation, of being themselves. And we hope that in your exploration you find the ability to simply, truly be yourself, and that you might come back and build the next generation of trans community and create the means for our liberation.

Thank you, and we hope to see you around!
