*so you want to get out of here. this hell.*

No worries! Depending on how the system is like, who you meet and how you present, getting out of this hell is as simple as learning rocket science.

Meaning, it’s not that simple.

### The basics
{: #h-d624208f-dbc6-4a6a-b1e7-697a3710e110}

The general guideline to be exempted from National Service for gender dysphoria, according to multiple first-hand sources, appears to be that:

1) *you have undergone hormone therapy for at least one year; and*

<div class="indented" markdown="1">

Anecdotal evidence points to this being a subjective evaluation (i.e. the one year not being a hard limit), that is most likely going to be based on whether you have some form of proof that you’ve done it.

The best proof appears to be a psychiatrist’s memo. It does not matter where you got it - CGH, NUH, IMH or private, as long as it states that you have been doing HRT. Other forms of proof also include spent medication (empty estrofem wheels) or just your E packaging itself.

</div>

2) *you have lived in a cross-gender role for at least one year.*

<div class="indented" markdown="1">

How they prove this appears to also be subjective. There are several factors to this, none of which are actually entirely needed, but will help in adding to the evidence that you *intend to transition* and are not fit for national service:

<div class="indented" markdown="1">

*A change in name.* This does not have to be a formal, legal change - merely your current name and how you are referred by friends.

*A change in how you present yourself* - again, this does not have to be something that you actively do in front of them. If you are undergoing efforts to change that presentation - for instance, voice training through a public or private speech therapist, you can show evidence of that as well.

</div>

However, you do not necessarily need to have actively made all of these steps - an intention to socially and medically transition may be sufficient. Medical officers have the option of assigning you a deferment or a temporary PES status as opposed to an immediate permanent exemption, so that they can prove that you have fulfilled the above two requirements down the road.

</div>

### What’s so complicated?
{: #h-0d415b2e-de03-4706-ae2f-1365bf8c16d6}

The complexity for this stems from how the above criteria are evaluated. It differs between medical officers, your enlistment status, where you have been posted to, (if applicable) your original Physical Employment Status (PES), etc.

Some of the variables that they may put into consideration:

1. How you will perform psychologically in service; i.e. signs of stuff like suicidal ideation, psychological states, etc. In essence, are you a good fit for service? Most likely, in cases of gender dysphoria (GD), this answer appears to be no.
2. Whether you intend to transition, even if you haven’t done so. Proving this may be simpler - again, the strongest evidence here appears to be a psychiatrist memo as it is one of the few steps people here take to get HRT.
3. What you may want. Pre-enlistment they do give you a choice to serve as a PES E enlistee, and some people do take it!
{: type="a"}

Some of the variables you will want to put into consideration:

1. Understand that the people you may interact with - including your officers, superiors and sometimes maybe subordinates - do not understand what trans women and non-binary people look like. To us, how you dress doesn’t matter; but it will factor for them.
2. The people you will interact with may have varied beliefs, but they may have an underlying understanding that there are people who want to get out of National Service and will take drastic steps to do so.
{: type="a"}

<mark class="highlight-yellow"><strong>Assume they do not understand mental health. Assume they do not have a concept of transgender issues. Assume they do not understand non-binary people. Assume they do not have a concept of gender dysphoria.</strong></mark> I would recommend that you mention as little as needed to anyone else who is not essential in the process of applying for exemption, especially if you have served.

### A glossary
{: #h-1f65918b-5af7-453d-81fa-d328d493d511}

#### PES Grades and what they mean
{: #h-ab89b58e-c0ec-47b4-b3d3-992334826a39}

- *PES A, B1*: Suitable for combat/frontline operational vocations
- *PES B2, B3, B4:* Suitable for some combat/frontline operational vocations and combat/frontline support vocations
- *PES BP:* BMT PES grade for people who are overweight. PES review will come after basic training (BMT, BRT, POBC) designed for overweight people.
- *PES C2, C9*: Suitable for some combat/frontline support and combat service support
- *PES D*: Temporarily unfit pending further review. PES D is temporary and only assigned to pre-enlistees pending further review - it should not be given to those who already have been assigned a different grade. On the rare chance it is, it’ll likely be clerical or administrative error.
- *PES E1, E9*: Suitable for combat service support and service vocations, or administrative support vocations
- *PES F*: ~~to pay respects~~ Medically unfit for service. (Effectively exempted from NS obligations)

PES grades from A to E can be temporary - a temporary grade means you will go through another medical review upon its expiry.

#### Common terms
{: #h-5b023d44-ef0b-48ec-9bd2-146b688c812d}

**MO**: Medical Officer - a military officer that is also a certified medical doctor. Some MOs are NSFs, while others are regulars.

**CMPB:** the Central Manpower Base, the NS unit that manages enlistment intake

**MINDEF PCC:** the Psychological Care Center. Handles psychological care for NSFs, regulars and NSmen.

**SAF Medical Board**: Chaired by a senior regular Medical Officer, the board handles and is in charge of [your PES reassignment](https://www.mindef.gov.sg/web/portal/mindef/news-and-events/latest-releases/article-detail/2018/july/11jul18_pq/!ut/p/z1/rZJNU9swEIZ_Sw8-KruynMjpzaUdaAcTSgPYujCOLX-ALTm2iMm_74b20M406TCDLhqtnt1995VAQQLKZLumylxjTdbSOVWLB7n6fHaBgX-1mq85Rnfr9eX809XXW7mAO1Cg-rwpIF1ynW8CLliuhWCBEAELRSaYXBQLXiyllCI80Llxvash7RpT6JLl1jhtnIe17bSHRk8jy0zB9I6io4dt5vTo2KBbnY2aAj7y0MPH53bvIee08_Ch38L9_6QqusYjK0LKV6_I6ntwzgP0L1fxzZwqLJfhGq99PJe_gRM1UtIgj2uQcL9r9AS3xg4dufvjjeZdIHx7lXBiSnqx5nG7VREZfXD2xUHyzk5TC3-Iz-KKxGeuZo0pLSQHGJIDDMkfMAmuWrv59ZcisxEhpQ261IMeZs8DhWvn-vGjhx5O0zSrrK1aPctt5-G_Umo70kR_k9B3XSj27KmMv4hAbfZTGbt5Gn34CULSHV0!/dz/d5/L2dBISEvZ0FBIS9nQSEh/?urile=wcm%3Apath%3A%2Fmindef-content%2Fhome%2Fnews-and-events%2Flatest-releases%2F2018%2Fjuly%2F11jul18_pq). **Specialist medical boards** handle cases that require further specialist opinion.

### For pre-enlistees
{: #h-53017a89-6786-44d0-88dc-e9156d5fc13c}

The pre-enlistment process looks like this:

<div class="table-wrap"><table>
<tr><th></th><th>Action</th><th>Remarks</th></tr>
<tr><th>1</th><td>Receive Notice for NS Registration letter</td><td>At 16.5 years of age.</td></tr>
<tr><th>2</th><td>Apply for deferment for studies (if applicable)</td><td>If not eligible, continue to step 3</td></tr>
<tr><th>3</th><td>Complete online pre-enlistment documentation, including:<br>- a Medical Screening Questionnaire<br>- booking a medical screening appointment</td><td>Once you are no longer eligible for deferment.</td></tr>
<tr><th>4</th><td>Medical screening</td><td>At Central Manpower Base (CMPB)</td></tr>
</table></div>

*NS Registration letter and deferment.* You would need to apply for deferment for studies and complete your pre-enlistment documentation as usual (because **it is a crime not to**).

*Online pre-enlistment documentation.* There is a Medical Screening Questionnaire as part of the online pre-enlistment documentation process. Note that your parents will be able to see and will need to acknowledge this submission. If you do not feel secure enough to declare that you have gender dysphoria during the medical screening questionnaire, you do not need to do so. You’ll also be asked to book an appointment for pre-enlistment medical screening at CMPB.

*Medical screening.* There will be a medical screening at the Central Manpower Base you are required to attend once you are no longer eligible for deferment and before enlistment. This is usually a one-day process for most cisgender males, but for anyone who has a health problem, you’ll be asked during the screening itself to return for an additional review and/or follow-up checks.

#### For the first medical checkup
{: #h-d8739f9d-37aa-4304-812f-7b9e816dec89}

Follow essentially what the [CMPB website says](https://www.cmpb.gov.sg/web/portal/cmpb/home/before-ns/pre-enlistment-process/medical-screening-and-psychometric-test/what-to-bring-for-medical-screening):

- Go eat before your medical screening appointment (as the process takes 4-5 hours).
- Bring two identity documents.
- Bring your medical documentation - this means stuff like a psychiatrist’s memo or proof that you’re undergoing HRT (if you started before 18) along.

You’ll be asked to have your picture taken (with uniform) and you will be asked to be measured for uniform sizes. You will be asked to take off your shirt. This would be a good start point for you to flag that you *don’t want to* because you have gender dysphoria, but there are also other points where you can do the same.

There will be seven stations. If you have medical documentation, you may be asked to hand them in; you can simply say you have a memo if you have one (and not specify).

You do not need a psychiatrist’s memo to prove that you have gender dysphoria; you can simply just declare. CMPB’s job is to make the evaluation anyway. *Anecdotal evidence has proven, however, that having a memo will help provide them with evidence that you are intending to transition or that you do have gender dysphoria.*

The stations are:

1. *Clinical laboratory station:* blood and urine testing
2. *Ear, nose and throat station*: hearing tests
3. *Eye station*: vision tests
4. *Dental station*: Oral examination and dental charting, with dental x-ray
5. *X-ray station*: Chest X-ray screening for assessing heart and lung condition.
6. *Clinical examination:* Height, weight, blood pressure and an ECG, and finally an examination and evaluation from an MO. **This is where you hand the documentation you may have, or declare that you have gender dysphoria.**

   1. You’ll be asked a list of questions here about gender dysphoria here, including some rather invasive ones - whether you are uncomfortable with your male body, cross dress, are on HRT, been bullied or are afraid of being bullied for gender non-conforming behaviour, and uncomfortable with showering nude with men
   {: type="a"}
7. *Psychometric testing*: a simple IQ test and survey testing for loyalty to the country, etc. Just answer honestly.

You’ll be asked to take off your shirt when you are being measured for uniform sizes, asked to take a chest X-ray, and at the clinical examination station. You will also be asked to show your genitalia at the clinical examination station.

At the end you may be handed an advisory note on further requirements. It’s likely that if you’ve declared that you have gender dysphoria you will be given a PES D status and asked to return for a psychological review through a Further Reporting Order. You may require an appointment to IMH or another hospital.

<aside class="callout callout-pink">
<div class="callout-icon" aria-hidden="true">🩺</div>
<div class="callout-body" markdown="1">

<u>Further Reporting Orders</u>

Further Reporting Orders will be sent to you in the mail, and you may be asked to go to the Medical Classification Centre (MCC) at CMPB - or a restructured hospital (likely IMH).

If you are sent to IMH, you will have to print out a letter of identity (LOI) which will be sent to you. You’ll need to go to the NS Portal and access the LOI. You’ll need to bear the medical fees if you do not print the LOI; as with all documents, you will be referred to by your legal name and gender.

The IMH review will be similar to that at the CMPB Medical Classification Centre.

</div>
</aside>

#### For a second review at CMPB’s Medical Classification Centre (or IMH)
{: #h-553e0b82-7a69-4e34-b8d2-acfe51daaa41}

<aside class="callout callout-purple">
<div class="callout-icon" aria-hidden="true">📖</div>
<div class="callout-body" markdown="1">

It is possible that you are given the option to serve. If you accept, you may be granted a PES E1 or E9 grade with a related vocation. <br><br>While this would be ultimately your own decision, multiple independent sources report the following challenges:<br>- a two-year gap you have to explain to others after transitioning<br>- you may have to deal with reservist, though this is unlikely at a PES E1 or PES E9 vocation<br>- you won’t be able to socially transition for two years<br>- you may experience sexual harassment and just regular harassment<br><br>These challenges were corroborated by multiple sources, reflecting on their personal experience. However, they may not be typical for all trans people. <br><br>There are other reasons why you may find serving beneficial: <br>- citizenship and ease of access for citizenship (though serving is not a factor in a citizenship application) <br>- the military can be a structured place for reflection<br>- interest in military and related industries<br>- a wish to gain experience or to use this as an opportunity as a break from study or work obligations

</div>
</aside>

You’ll be given an appointment date via an SMS saying that you’ll need to see the SAF psychiatrist at CMPB; there’s a huge chance you’ll be deadnamed there as with most interactions with the medical establishment (as long as your legal name isn’t changed).

At the review, you’ll be asked the standard questions:

<div class="indented" markdown="1">

1. When did you first experience gender dysphoria?
2. Are you out to your parents or siblings?

   1. **This is important** - parents will counter that their children do not have any health issues if they object (or don’t know that you are trans), or write letters to try to counter MINDEF’s exemption. Please let them know if your parents object to what is, to MINDEF, a mental health issue.
   {: type="a"}
3. What are your current plans (study, work, etc)?
4. What is your preferred name, and why did you choose that name?
5. Are you okay with serving National Service under a limited role?
6. What are the medications that you are taking now?
7. Who are you seeing for your gender dysphoria?

</div>

This is also a point where you can reveal if you are going to other appointments - like for voice training via speech therapy. CMPB does have access to appointments that you may have made with CGH (or NUH, or IMH) but it is not updated.

If you are taking self-managed or DIY HRT at this point, you should bring along your medication as further justification.

At the end you may be issued a letter for deferment, and you may be sent a notice for exemption if you are deemed to have met the criteria for exemption with gender dysphoria.

### Between the pre-enlistee stage and enlistment
{: #h-677ae950-6111-4062-ae65-64f23bcbc535}

If you have completed your pre-enlistee medical screening, and you have documentation related to your own gender dysphoria (like a doctor’s memo) you can either opt to walk in to the CMPB or email contact@ns.sg with your doctor’s memo.

You may be asked to attend a further medical review - you’ll be notified and sent a Further Reporting Order by mail (check the [second review section](#h-553e0b82-7a69-4e34-b8d2-acfe51daaa41) for more). If your PES status changes, you’ll be informed by SMS; if your enlistment date is postponed, they will send you a letter stating so as well.

The review usually takes a month, though they will assist to expedite the submission process.

<div class="callout-icon" aria-hidden="true">‼️</div>

There is no choice, and you will have to enlist. See [the next section](#h-31f21554-18c1-43bc-9290-b820b6455225) for details.

### For people currently in service

First of all - we are so incredibly sorry. Being in service and trying to declare and be exempted from gender dysphoria will be a lot more difficult.

#### What you need
{: #h-61565563-df34-443f-b61a-1782bc6dde22}

<figure class="image"><a href="/assets/notion/national-service/Untitled.png"><img src="/assets/notion/national-service/Untitled.png" alt="" width="384" loading="lazy"></a></figure>

You need a huge dose of community and emotional support. It’s hopefully something we hope you can find on Transcend; being in the service can be so alienating and you may end up experiencing sexual (and regular) harassment, on top of familial and social resistance.

**Understand that unless they are explicitly supportive, your superiors, squadmates or bunkmates are not your allies.** This isn’t to say that you are going to immediately hate-crimed once you come out; anecdotally there is evidence to suggest that commanders, counsellors and medical officers are at least understanding in Tekong and other areas. It is safe to however assume that cis people don’t usually understand what you are going through, and support from them may be sparse or incomplete.

<aside class="callout callout-purple">
<div class="callout-icon" aria-hidden="true">💊</div>
<div class="callout-body" markdown="1">

<u>Can you use your 11B to pay for HRT in public healthcare?<br><br></u>Surprisingly, the answer seems to be an unambiguous yes, but it’s likely that the three services - which often connotes medical transition with social transition - will exempt you when you try to start doing this. You’ll need to obtain approval from the Psychological Care Centre at Kent Ridge (as well as your parents if you’re under 21), and you’ll still be unable to socially transition at all if you start.<br><br>Your 11B will cover costs such as bloodwork with an endocrinologist, speech therapy and voice training, and sessions at psychological medicine if you’re still in service. Still, it’s possible you may be cut off; audits might flag the expenditure. <br><br>Inconsistency is to be expected, however - if your attending psychiatrist changes at the Psychological Care Centre (where you would go to, to start a medical review to be exempted) it may be likely you might be given that exemption from NS and be discharged (or that you already might fit one of the two criteria above and they’d exempt you anyway).

</div>

#### Getting exempted
{: #h-0d7eb0d9-3194-4e5c-8656-c7e5ef82f7d6}

The chief method of getting exempted here seems to be through a medical review.

This can be achieved through the following:

In BMT (or police/civil defence equivalent training), you can report sick and visit the MO. At Pulau Tekong, the place to do so will be at the in-camp counsellor in CareHub (School I). We do not know whether there is an equivalent in SPF NS or SCDF NS basic training at the moment, but the process should be similar.

You’ll then have specialist appointments at the Institute of Mental Health and/or Psychological Care Center at MMI Kent Ridge. You can choose to go to a private psychiatrist to obtain a memo indicating that you have gender dysphoria here; this will aid in any medical review that you have at PCC Kent Ridge.

There is also evidence to suggest that gender dysphoria as a condition can and will be evaluated by general psychiatrists at IMH, from experiences by other servermates, but it may be hard to use this to get HRT from the public healthcare route.

The wait time to get a medical review may vary but will be long - one servermate reported a length of eight months.

If you wish to medically transition and want to start the process of doing so, you can attempt to get a referral to gender care at CGH or NUH at this point. Take a look at the [gender-affirming healthcare guide]({{site.baseurl}}/guide/gender-affirming-healthcare/#h-830fc323-de70-4410-bfd6-a653a36b81e3) if you wish to do so. However, it is unlikely that you will be able to socially transition until you leave the armed forces.

If you are in BMT, there is a chance that you may be down PESed and sent to a PES C or PES E vocation.

You may be asked to go to regular reviews at PCC Kent Ridge (or equivalent) once you have been down PESed and sent to a non-combat vocation. If it is clear that you are experiencing psychological distress from serving it will be likely that you will be given some form of exemption, though as a rule it is going to be harder to obtain.

### For people who have ORDed
{: #h-b2a70afd-e2bf-408f-8963-8c6cc14349ab}

So you’re out of the military. It is generally simpler for you to obtain a deferment or exemption from this point on, but this honestly depends on your unit and their attitudes, as well as the medical center you may be assigned to.

You shouldn’t be called back within the first or second year after your service, and some units have a policy to not call back people who are still in university or completing their tertiary studies. You should not rely on exemptions for full-time studies after this however, as there will be a possibility you may be called up during term break.

Depending on when your reservist cycle is set to begin, you can do the following:

- Obtain a psychiatrist memo. In the public health system, this is likely to take more than six to eight months from initial referral from polyclinic. You can also opt to obtain a memo from a private psychiatrist. Take a look at the [gender-affirming healthcare guide for details]({{site.baseurl}}/guide/gender-affirming-healthcare/).
- Book an appointment for a medical review on the OneNS portal, [here](https://www.ns.gov.sg/web/profiles/nsman/health-and-medical#medical-review). The review may be scheduled at different medical centers depending on where you are posted; this information will be given to you on portal itself.

  - Present the memo to the medical officer that will see you. Alternatively, if you do not have a memo, you can present evidence that you are medically transitioning - such as appointments with the endocrinologist, evidence that you have taken HRT, and future appointments with a psychiatrist.
  - There’s a chance that you may be asked to go meet with the medical board which decides your PES status. This is less likely now, but if this happens, ask when this may be in case you need to ask for leave from work or school.
  - The medical officer may recommend to the medical board a temporary PES status - E1 or E9 (per protocol). The temporary PES status may not stop the unit from calling you back, so this means you may have to apply for deferment.
  - They may also recommend a PES F, even if you have not met the ‘one year’ criteria. This is perhaps based on the subjective idea of how you look, and whether you have taken actual steps to transition. Socially transitioning is not a necessary step at your medical review, but it may be helpful; keep in mind that they’re looking for an intent to transition.
  - At the end of your appointment, you should request for a memo <u>no matter how soon the MO says the medical board meeting will be</u>.

    - Use this memo to apply for deferment if you need to, in case the medical board meeting is delayed or in case your case is not heard at the medical board in time. Deferments are processed by your unit, however, and you should only do this if you absolutely need to - if reservist is less than two weeks away, for instance.
- If your reservist cycle is less than two months away, you can apply for deferment with a psychiatrist’s memo. Do note that deferments are processed by your unit - your commanding officer will be the one making the final decision on this, and they may not defer you if you do not have a valid reason.

  - You should proceed to book an appointment for a medical review after your deferment, or after your reservist cycle.
  - If you do not have your deferment approved, it is possible for you to report sick on the first day and hand a psychiatrist’s memo to the medical officer. This is however still a rather risky process.

<u>On mobilisation call ups</u>

You should not be penalised if you have had a mobilisation call up and have long hair or issues with your attire as it is only limited to one day. This doesn’t preclude people questioning stuff like hair and piercings, however, but your unit officers and NCOs understand that people have lives outside.

If you’ve had a bad experience with a mobilisation call up - let us know so we can update the guide.
