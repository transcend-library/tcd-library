Looking for a new gender-affirming hairstyle? Here are a few tips, alongside a few places that you can first try out something that works for you.

These things technically *also apply* to everyone (cis or trans) to be honest, as long as you want a new hairstyle!

The stylists recommended here are more conscious of how you want to look gender-wise, and can be trusted to give you something that’s truly gender-affirming, but it might help to follow these steps:

#### 1) Have a view of what you might want!
{: #h-0e525ebf-d010-4e64-b6c1-a73f47ec0e5f}

This is the most important step, and it means having a mood board. Here’s an example of a Pinterest board you can use: [https://pin.it/1QLIvgm](https://pin.it/1QLIvgm)

<figure class="image"><a href="/assets/notion/haircuts/Screenshot_2022-08-01_at_01.33.09.png"><img src="/assets/notion/haircuts/Screenshot_2022-08-01_at_01.33.09.png" alt="" width="2166" loading="lazy"></a></figure>

You can also use other apps or just have a random collection of pictures you have on your phone, but Pinterest seems to be the easiest way to discover similar looks.

This does one of two things:

<div class="indented" markdown="1">

1. it helps with determining exactly what you want, perhaps in the future.

   We’re not sure if this works with long hair! But perhaps having it trimmed to ensure that it’s structured in a way that you want to look when it grows out - to avoid, partly, the awkward growing out stage - will be helpful. Some stylists (e.g. Dolly’s) will ask you if you are intending to keep it long.
2. It helps with communicating exactly what you want, too. Having enough references - at least 10 - will help stylists picture what you want.
{: type="a"}

</div>

#### 2) Keep your hair as long as you can (if you want to)
{: #h-6aad7809-d5b5-4b58-bd6a-1c1c7d6f230d}

This means giving the stylist enough material to work with, especially if you want a style that is slightly longer than what your birth gender would usually have.

If you can’t do that for long, try to just simply push it off for as long as possible. (I’m writing from a transfeminine lens here; if you’re transmasculine, you can opt to just cut your hair.)

A simple and effective tip, assuming you’re relatively independent and not in school or National Service, is to say you don’t have the time to cut your hair.

#### 3) Ask for, and understand hair care tips
{: #h-e4a0fdad-fe7b-4b90-81ff-20f8a201ec89}

Most experienced stylists will have some understanding on how you should care for your hair. Some may recommend blowdrying, others might recommend towel drying, but these things are dependent on how your hair is personally.

You don’t need to purchase the products they recommend (for Dolly’s - she won’t recommend a particular product; I’m not sure if the others will do so) - just the types of hair care product that fit best for your hair.

Shampooing is important in our humid weather. Purchase shampoos that do not contain sulphates as sulphates can strip your hair of its natural oils. You can opt to condition your hair occasionally and shampoo daily, though if you leave the house and sweat often shampooing daily is advised.

#### 4) Get hair accessories if you’re keeping your hair long
{: #h-fb80e983-1b22-4f0a-9e36-55145be1c932}

<figure class="image"><a href="/assets/notion/haircuts/Screenshot_2022-08-01_at_02.44.43.png"><img src="/assets/notion/haircuts/Screenshot_2022-08-01_at_02.44.43.png" alt="" width="1144" loading="lazy"></a></figure>

Simple bobby pins or hair clips will do, if you need them, as well as a simple headband that you can get at any store! These will keep your hair out of your face or ears.

If you want a ponytail, a hair tie will do; don’t use a rubber band because that may put unnecessary tension on your hair.

<u>Hairstyling - clay v. gel v. pomade v. wax<br><br></u>For the Dudes - there are several hair products that you can choose from asides from the gel you see from the stupid little Gatsby commercial (*when you’re happy and you know it clap your hair?*) in the late 00s. <br><br>All four of them - clay, gel, pomade and wax - offer different degrees of hold (how well your hair stays in place) and shine (how much gloss they offer). **Hold** refers to how much lift and volume you can get from how well your hair stays in one place. **Shine** refers to how glossy or matte your hair can get with the product, which might help if you want more natural-looking hair. <br><br>**Pomades** - if you have thick hair, this is for you; pomades offer a lot of shine but not too much hold. If you have thinning or thin hair, however, the dense texture will tend to clump hair together and you’ll end up making your hair look thinner.<br><br>**Wax** - if you have curly hair, this is for you; wax offer less shine and less hold; it has less tendency to clump your curls and will make your hair look more relaxed. Wax is similar to pomade - you shouldn’t use wax if you have thin hair. Using clay would be better in this case.<br><br>**Gel** - offers high shine and high hold. Used to be popular in the 90s. Using gel every day may strip your hair of its natural oils and leave it weaker. <br><br>**Clay** - most versatile. Adds volume and texture, and can come in a variety of hold strength so you can try to tailor it to a specific hairstyle you’re looking for. Most clays have a matte finish, so if you’re looking for some shine, clay is probably not for you.

</div>
</aside>

#### 5) Bring wet wipes in exceptionally humid weather (which is… just our weather)
{: #h-4535404f-0c66-45e2-8205-2913f1aad18a}

If you’re used to those \$12 cut and blow-dry spaces, you might have had the awkward experience of having a blow-dry machine brush across your face, walked out into the humid weather, brush your hair or touch your face and find tiny hairs that they’ve not managed to clean!

A \$50 cut and wash at Kimage and you might find the same problem. Just bring a wet wipe in exceptionally humid weather you can use to clean your face with after, and bring a spare mask.
